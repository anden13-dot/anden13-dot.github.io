Reading Into It

A Choose-Your-Own-Adventure Book spanning multiple physical mediums versed in psychological attachment theory.

Facts and Roles

Term: Spring 2026 Capstone Project

My responsibilities:

  • Narrative lead
  • Chief researcher on psychological concepts
  • Key user researcher
  • Made physical newspapers and blueprints

Group: Anden Benner and Theresa Vazaphilly

My partner’s responsibilities:

  • Project manager: setting & tracking deadlines
  • Art lead
  • Refined writing after criticism
  • Primary researcher on physical media and similar projects

Research

At the start of the course, I designed a Google Survey and asked my classmates and peers how they would feel and react in specific relationship scenarios.

The goal was to ground the story in situations readers could strongly relate to. The spread of answers confirmed our initial story beats, and I wrote the first draft of the narrative from there.

The attachment-style concepts came separately, from my research on psychological concepts.

We received about 37 initial responses, the majority of which encouraging us by providing a strong statistical basis for our narrative to be able to connect with as many individuals as possible.

Workshops and their Changes

Workshop 1

Heard

Our first draft on telling the story had issue with readers embodying a third person character.

Changed

We rewrote the narrative to have a second person point of view and modified the wording appropriately.

Workshop 2

Heard

Our proposed comic book format was not leveraging the medium enough and was found lacking in panel design.

Changed

We consulted Scott McCloud’s “Understanding Comics: The Invisible Art” and adjusted our panel and page design to better utilize the more creative medium.

Workshop 3

Heard

While our project was coming together nicely, workshop participants wanted to see more substance added to our project.

Changed

We adjusted the narrative so as to include a prototyped third path and physical materials as architectural blueprints in time for the showcase.

Three Paths, Three Mediums

Limits

Neither of us were particularly proficient in the physical aspect of the project and were constrained by the semester long time limit.

Reflection

I am incredibly proud of how we were able to make a far fetched idea to merge two of my majors, Psychology and Digital Narrative & Interactive Design, into a strong cohesive physical project that accomplishes its original goal to afford a safe exploration of the self.
